# Explainer

Short animated explainer films for non-technical viewers. The work runs in a fixed order, and each stage is reviewed before the next one starts.

## The rule

Visuals only start once there is a story. Do not draw, paint, render or animate anything until stages 1 and 2 are written down and agreed. If someone asks for visuals first, write the story and beats with them before going further.

## Workflow

### 1. Story

Settle these four things before anything else:

- **One idea.** Each film explains a single idea. If there are two, make two films.
- **The audience.** Assume non-technical people. No jargon unless the film defines it on screen.
- **A central metaphor.** One familiar thing that stands in for the idea and carries the whole film.
- **The takeaway.** The single thing the viewer should understand by the end, written as one plain sentence.

Detail and a worked example: [references/story-first.md](references/story-first.md).

### 2. Script beats

Write a beat list. For each beat, give:

- the voice-over line (draft wording; it is rewritten to fit the picture in stage 5)
- what moves on screen, and what that movement shows

Motion carries information: nothing moves unless it explains something. If a beat has movement that does not explain anything, cut the movement or cut the beat.

Detail: [references/story-first.md](references/story-first.md) and [references/techniques.md](references/techniques.md).

### 3. Blockout frames

Make a rough grey frame for each beat: simple shapes, placement, scale and camera framing only. No colour, texture or finishing.

Stop here and share the blockouts for review. Nothing is painted or rendered until the blockouts are approved.

### 4. Paint and motion

Once the blockouts are approved:

- paint the backdrops and characters
- set the camera moves and transitions
- animate each beat as written in the beat list
- apply the house style, one finishing method at a time

House settings and look rules: [references/house-style.md](references/house-style.md).
How and when to use each technique: [references/techniques.md](references/techniques.md).

### 5. Sound

- Lock the picture first.
- Write the final voice-over against the locked picture, then record it.
- Add sound effects, then a music bed under the whole film.

Detail: [references/sound-and-voice.md](references/sound-and-voice.md).

### 6. Checks

Before handing anything over, render and look at sampled frames (at least one per beat, plus the first and last frame). Do not hand over a film you have not watched.

Verify:

- [ ] The takeaway sentence from stage 1 is clearly made by the end.
- [ ] Every beat in the beat list is in the film, in order.
- [ ] Nothing moves without explaining something.
- [ ] House settings match [references/house-style.md](references/house-style.md) exactly.
- [ ] Shapes are cut-paper with sharp corners; no glows or glints.
- [ ] Text and labels sit on solid panels, never straight over a grid.
- [ ] Labels reveal with a stepped wipe rather than popping in.
- [ ] Foreground objects stay anchored to their ground plane during parallax moves.
- [ ] Voice-over matches what is on screen at each moment.
- [ ] Music sits low and runs continuously; effects are real recordings.
- [ ] No frames are blank, cropped wrongly or left over from an earlier version.
