# Techniques

Each technique below is its own step. The finishing methods (edge roughness, film grain, halftone, lens blur and fringing) are applied and checked one at a time, never bundled into a single "texture" step. That way each can be tuned or switched off without affecting the others. Exact values are in [house-style.md](house-style.md).

## Motion carries information

Nothing moves unless the movement explains something: a thing arriving, growing, splitting, travelling, being left out.

**When to use:** always. This is the test for every beat. If you cannot say what a movement explains, remove it. Idle drifting, bobbing and decorative loops are cut.

## Annotate and highlight

Draw attention to one part of the frame with a label, arrow, underline, outline or a dimming of everything else.

**When to use:** when the voice-over names something specific in a busy frame, or when the viewer needs to compare two parts. Highlight one thing at a time. Labels sit on solid panels and reveal with a stepped wipe (see [house-style.md](house-style.md)).

## Camera-blur transitions

Move between scenes with a fast camera move that blurs in the direction of travel, landing in the next scene.

**When to use:** when moving from one part of the story to the next, or between two places in the metaphor. It keeps the film continuous. Use a plain cut when the change is meant to feel abrupt.

## 2.5D parallax

Separate a scene into flat layers at different depths and move the camera so nearer layers shift more than distant ones.

**When to use:** to give a scene depth during a slow camera move, or to lead the eye towards the subject. Keep foreground objects anchored to the ground plane they sit on, so they do not appear to slide or float as the camera moves.

## Edge roughness

Break up perfectly clean vector edges so shapes read as cut paper.

**When to use:** on all painted shapes and characters, as its own finishing pass. Check it at full size; too much makes small shapes look chewed.

## Film grain

A layer of moving grain over the whole frame.

**When to use:** as a finishing pass on every shot, so flat colour areas do not look digital. Rotate the grain plates so the grain moves rather than sitting still on the image.

## Halftone

A dot pattern in shaded areas, like printed paper.

**When to use:** as a finishing pass to give shading a printed feel. Check it does not shimmer on fine detail or small text.

## Lens blur and fringing

Slight blur towards the frame edges, colour fringing at high-contrast edges, and mild barrel distortion.

**When to use:** as the last finishing pass, to make the frame feel filmed through a lens. Keep the centre of the frame sharp, and check that labels near the edges are still readable.
