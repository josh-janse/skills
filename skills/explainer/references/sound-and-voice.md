# Sound and voice

Sound is usually the last stage, starting once the picture is locked. The voice-over can also come first (see below).

## Voice-over

- Record the voice-over either way round. Recorded after the footage, the script follows the picture. Recorded first, the scene times come from the pauses in the recording, as in the painterly kit.
- Start from the draft lines in the beat list, then rewrite each one against the locked picture so the words land when the matching thing appears on screen.
- Read the final script aloud against the picture before recording.
- ElevenLabs works well for generating the voice.
- If a line does not fit its beat, change the line, not the picture.

## Sound effects

- Use real recordings released under CC0, for example from Freesound, rather than synthesised placeholder sounds.
- Keep a list of each effect used with its source link, in case the licence needs checking later.
- Add effects only where something on screen happens that a sound would help explain.

## Music

- Use a continuous rhythm bed, mixed low, under the whole film.
- Do not write hits that mirror each visual event ("mickey-mousing").
- Do not limit music to the pauses in the voice-over; that pattern soon feels predictable.
- The voice-over must stay clear over the music at all times.
